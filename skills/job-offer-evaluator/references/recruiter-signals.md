# Recruiter-side signals

Read the message itself for these before judging the role. Each flag is a reason to ask a question, not an automatic reject.

- **Identity of the end client**: named, or hidden ("a large US-based technology company")? Hidden is normal at staffing/outsourcing firms, but it means you cannot judge the work or the client's stability.
- **Who the recruiter is**: "Talent Acquisition Sourcer" at a consultancy/body-shop sending templated outreach differs from an in-house hiring manager. Sourcers often have no say on pay or scope.
- **Template and persistence**: identical text re-sent ("busy or not interested?"), generic praise ("your expertise is required"), no reference to anything specific in your profile. Low personalisation means volume outreach.
- **Salary wording**: "up to X" is a ceiling, not an offer. "Competitive" or missing means unknown. A concrete band with currency, period (hourly/annual) and contract type is a good sign.
- **Contract form vs the role**: B2B for a "long-term" single-client role can signal disguised employment (false autónomo risk) and shifts holidays, sickness and notice risk onto you.
- **Currency/region mismatch**: pay in USD for work in Spain, or a US client paying a Spanish contractor through an intermediary; check who invoices whom and where VAT applies.
- **Scope vs title**: A "Senior Engineer" title whose duties are mostly testing, evaluation or labelling work is narrower than the title suggests.
- **Urgency and vagueness**: "quick chat", no job description, no team, no duration.
- **Reputation**: search for the company and the end client (layoffs, Glassdoor-type reviews, reputation of the staffing firm with contractors).

Report a short list of the flags found (and the good signs), and turn the most important unknowns into recruiter questions.
